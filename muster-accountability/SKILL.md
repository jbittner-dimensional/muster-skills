# Personnel Accountability

This is how we account for our people. Follow it in order. Every step is a
question the graph can already answer — you are reading the fused record, not
guessing.

## 1. Establish the picture
Pull the enterprise status. For every affected station, note confirmed vs.
unconfirmed head-counts and the timestamp of the oldest source feeding them. A
number with no time behind it is not accountability — it is a rumor.

## 2. Work the unconfirmed, not the confirmed
Sort each station's roster by muster status. Spend attention on the people who
are **unconfirmed** or **unreachable**. A confirmed person needs nothing from
you; an unconfirmed one needs a decision.

## 3. When two systems disagree, do not pick — surface
If the warehouse says a person is at one station and the movement system says
another, show **every** version with its origin and its age. Decide with a
written rationale. The graph never picks a winner; the certified policy does.
Once you have decided the same way twice, codify it so the next hundred
conflicts resolve the way you just taught it.

## 4. On an emergency indicator, act in seconds
If a tracked movement squawks an emergency, treat everyone associated with it as
unconfirmed immediately, draft the recall, and route it for a human to send.
Speed here is the whole job.

## 5. Households and dependents count too
Accountability is not just badge-holders. Capture dependents and pets by
household — an evacuation planner needs the real number of bodies and animals,
not the roster count.

## 6. Close the loop every morning
Produce a 0600 rollup: what changed overnight, who is still unconfirmed, and the
single next action for each open name. Hand it to the officer at login so the
day starts from truth, not from an inbox.
